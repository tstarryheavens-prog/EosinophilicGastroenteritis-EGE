1. The Post-FIT Economic Reality in Residential Energy

Under Japan’s Feed-in Tariff (FIT) framework, early solar adopters enjoyed premium export buyback rates of 31.0 to 48.0 JPY/kWh. However, upon post-FIT expiration, buyback rates collapse to a nominal 7.0–8.5 JPY/kWh. Concurrently, grid import tariffs have climbed to 36.0 JPY/kWh during daytime peak hours.

Under this stark price disparity (selling at ~8 JPY while buying at ~36 JPY), exporting residential surplus solar power is economically irrational. Maximizing on-site self-consumption via high-capacity battery storage and intelligent load management represents the only sustainable financial strategy.

📊 The Tariff Differential
• Night Tariff (01:00 - 06:00): 16.3 JPY / kWh
• Daytime Grid Tariff: 36.0 JPY / kWh (+120.8% markup)
• Surplus Export Buyback (Post-FIT): 8.5 JPY / kWh
Economic Directive: Never buy power at 36.0 JPY; charge at 16.3 JPY only when solar deficit is mathematically guaranteed.

3. The Just-In-Time (JIT) Dynamic Charging Algorithm

Fixed night charging schedules suffer from a fundamental failure mode: charging a 14.9kWh battery to 100% using night grid electricity leaves zero headroom for next-day surplus solar generation, forcing cheap solar energy to be spilled or sold back to the grid for pennies.

Our predictive controller executes at 23:30 nightly using the following logic: 

# Predictive JIT Night Charge Optimizer
def calculate_optimal_night_charge(current_soc, weather_forecast, household_history):
    # Predict tomorrow's solar generation from solar radiation API (W/m^2)
    predicted_solar_kwh = model_solar_generation(weather_forecast)
    # Estimate baseline household consumption (refrigerators, HVAC, compute servers)
    predicted_load_kwh = model_consumption(household_history, weather_forecast["temperature"])
    
    expected_surplus_kwh = max(0, predicted_solar_kwh - predicted_load_kwh)
    battery_usable_capacity = 14.9 * 0.90 # 90% DoD buffer
    
    # Target night SoC preserves exact headroom for predicted afternoon solar peak
    required_night_kwh = (predicted_load_kwh - predicted_solar_kwh) + RESERVE_BUFFER
    target_soc = min(95.0, max(25.0, (required_night_kwh / battery_usable_capacity) * 100))
    
    return target_soc
